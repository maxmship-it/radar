The fix list
Ten changes for the next videos, in order of impact. Packaging first, then script.

   1   Let every split test run until YouTube calls it, up to 14 days.
   Tests ran a median 9.1 hours. Identical variants landed up to 13.0 points apart. Only 23 of 174 tests cleared
   that.

   2   Test one change at a time, and test big ideas, not capitals or brackets.
   Capitals, brackets, numbers and length all sat inside ±2 points. Later rounds with new ideas cleared the noise
   13 times against 5.

   3   Use “It’s Boring, But…” (or “It Sounds INSANE, But…”) as a title option on every how-to video.
   Led 19 of 20 tests across five videos. Put it back on the insulin video, where it led five rounds.

   4   Keep making food-industry exposés the main format.
   Median 274,842 engaged views, about double any other group, and 55.3% of all views.

   5   Thumbnails: one real brand, person or comparison. No big-text-only designs and no busy
   collages.
   Comparison layouts led 6 clear tests. Big text only never led. Collages lost both times.

   6   Open on a specific event, person or document in the first sentence. Skip the montage of
   strangers’ clips.
   Specific openers kept 63.0% at one minute. Clip montages kept 56.3%.

   7   Script for reveals. Cut long mechanism explanations to one clear line plus a visual.
   Reveals were holds 18 of 18 times. Study and mechanism stretches were dips 31 of 40.

   8   Drop hedges and “the final thing” signposts. Tease the next point as a reveal instead.
   Hedges were dips 9 of 9. List-item changes were dips 8 of 10.

   9   Make sponsor reads shorter and tie them to the story.
   Reads cost a median 6.1 points against 2.4 normally. 9 of 12 show viewers skipping them.

   10   End every video by naming the next one, before the sign-off.
   6.63% median end-screen clicks with it, 2.59% without.

What was analyzed, where it came from and what it cannot tell you.

   About this data
   27 long-form videos published from 9 March to 21 November 2026, from Max’s YouTube Studio export
   (10 March to 21 November). One untitled export row with no publish date and a 17:43 video missing from
   the export were left out.
   Every comparison across videos uses engaged views, because YouTube changed how it counts views
   on 27 August 2026. Money is in NZD.
   Split tests: 176 screenshots (499 readings) grouped into 174 unique tests, plus 48 Studio tests and 2
   vidIQ-only tests, minus 11 overlaps: 213 in all. 11 tests belong to “If I Only Had 2 Weeks To Kill Visceral
   Fat”, which Max confirmed was taken down, so it has no Studio or retention data.
   Retention: YouTube’s 1% grid (100 points per video, 2,700 in all). The cortisol belly video’s curve covers
   about a third of its views so far.
   Transcripts came from TubeAI, because YouTube blocked downloads from the machine this was built
   on. They have no timestamps, so every transcript time is estimated from word position. The walking
   video (6vmm1Fw3ywI) only returned an AI summary and has no transcript analysis. The PFAS video
   (VBeerzA1Vso) was cut at 5,000 words, its last six percent.
   Comments: 796 top comments on eight videos and 200 recent comments (17 spam removed), each
   sorted by theme by a reader working to one fixed codebook. Thumbnail features, intro types and
   moment types were coded by hand.
   Checked and left out because it showed nothing or could not be measured: new versus returning
   viewers (not exported), per-video traffic sources, day-by-day click-through rate, likes versus dislikes
   (93.6% to 99.3% likes on every video, no pattern).
   Nothing here is an experiment except the split tests, and they were short. Everything else is correlation.
   27 videos is a small sample. Treat groups under 10, and gaps inside the noise, as hints.
